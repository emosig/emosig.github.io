About

I am a PhD candidate in mathematics at the Università di Pisa, where I also hold a research fellowship in the project OTODDLE (Optimal Transport and Large Deviations for Deep Learning Theory), led by Dario Trevisan and funded by the Italian Ministry of University and Research.

My work sits between probability theory and applied algebraic topology. I study how large random systems, such as wide neural networks, behave in their scaling limits, and how topological descriptors like persistent homology can be compared, computed and made stable.

Research

Quantitative limits of neural networks

I prove convergence rates for neural networks in large-width regimes. With Andrea Agazzi and Dario Trevisan, I obtained the first quantitative bound, in quadratic Wasserstein distance, on how close a trained wide network is to its Gaussian-process limit at any positive training time. Later work extends this to general architectures through tensor programs, and to deep transformers, where token trajectories converge to a stochastic interacting particle system that synchronizes when the common noise is strong enough.

Multiparameter persistence

I study distances between multiparameter persistence modules. In joint work, we showed that the matching distance is attained on a structured set of parameters described by the extended Pareto grid, a Morse-theoretic invariant, and introduced the convex matching distance, which preserves the smoothness of the input and can be used in gradient-based optimization.

Group equivariant operators

My work on multiparameter persistence fits into the theory of group equivariant non-expansive operators (GENEOs), developed as a way to build prescribed symmetries directly into topological descriptors. The operator families behind both the classical and the convex matching distance are particular cases of GENEOs.
